# FAQ

## Is it out yet?

Not yet. Cartomancy is still in development, with no public release.

## Do I need to know tarot or astrology?

No. Start with the picture: the Fool leaps, the Hanged Man suspends, the Tower falls. A card shows its favour, and the **Because** explains what the sky and your surroundings contributed. The **Commonplace** fills with what you learn as you play.

## Where should I begin?

A village cartographer sells a Battered Fool for an emerald. You can also fish, explore or make a blank from paper and an ink sac. Hold a blank with the Astrolabe and read a place or creature to print a Minor. Your first card doesn't need to be the Fool.

## Will casting use up my favourite card?

No. Casting never uses up a card; its ink just needs time to dry. You can keep extra copies for spells and other workings, or deliberately use one up in soup or tinctures. The cauldron asks again before taking a protected find, such as a special-paper card or your last copy.

## Why does the same card behave differently today?

The Moon, House and weekday have moved; your ground or company may have changed too. Look at the Because. The card names its result **Exalted, Favoured, Plain, Ashen** or **Fallen**. Your stance also matters, and the Cut's foretold face is shown before you commit.

## Is a reversed card a failed spell?

It has another working. Reversed Death briefly stops nearby creatures dying, enemies included. Reversed Tower protects a place from explosions while preventing your own block-breaking there. At the table you can lay a card contrary to choose its shadow for certain, at a longer rest.

## What is the difference between a spread and a fortune?

Cards you lay by hand compose a spell. Cards dealt from your Folio tell a fortune about a subject. Study spends nothing; the knock commits the reading. [The table](the-table.md) explains both.

## Does it tell my real fortune?

It reads your Minecraft world. A Sword can point towards an unvisited structure; a Cup can report the weather's current schedule. It says when the reading was true and distinguishes what is confirmed from what is predicted or beyond its sight. It makes no claims about your life outside the game.

## Can I play alone?

Yes. You can read for yourself and play Card-Jitsu against the Astronomer or a villager you have traded with. A traded villager or the Astronomer can also stand in for the Friend lesson. None of that needs another player to be online.

## What can I do with friends?

Read for someone who cuts the deck and give them a Cartouche of what was told. Share soup or ladle a pot of Starlight Chowder. Play Card-Jitsu, open the sanctum's dial to company, or pair Mercury jacks to post parcels between bases. Everyone shares the sky; each reader brings their own cards and history to it.

## Does missing a night set me back?

No. The Great Year brings its Houses round again, and the Almanac shows what's coming. There's no daily streak or mana pool to refill. Some workings cost experience or goods, but missing an evening costs nothing.

## Can my cat help?

Entrust a suitable card to your own tamed animal and it becomes a familiar. A sitting cat with the Empress tends the crops nearby; a following familiar answers occasions suited to its card. Preview the binding first. Its casts share the card's rest with you, and you can take the card back.

## Can I lose everything by breaking a Snowglobe?

A globe broken without Silk Touch shatters and seals its sanctum. The contents stay inside. Recover the shattered globe with Silk Touch and restore it at an Orrery. Moving a whole globe safely also takes experience points.

## What is the graduation music disc?

***In Good Company*** is the music disc the Quire gives you at graduation. The [included recording (Rendition 1)](../media/music/in-good-company.ogg) is still open to feedback.

## What happens when I collect all seventy-eight?

Bring the complete Folio to an Orrery or Reader's Table for the Living Codex ceremony. The Grand Reading tells your own journey back, using the cards you found and played. Even the Battered card that started it has a place in the reading.
