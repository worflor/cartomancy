# The Moon Garden

*A pond, a stone lantern, somewhere to sit. Enough to begin receiving company.*

## Make somewhere to look up

The Moon Garden borrows the Edo moon-viewing garden's stone, water and quiet seat. Its heart is the **Stone Lantern**. Use it with an empty hand and it reads the ground within eight blocks, briefly outlining what it finds.

It asks for four qualities: open sky, still water, growing things and somewhere to sit. A stair, slab or carpet can serve as a seat. The Voice names what is missing: *"There is no water to hold the moon."* Once the place has all four, it is ready to receive a guest.

The plants also give it a character. Poppies and sunflowers favour Fire; cornflowers and lily pads Water; white flowers and bamboo Air; moss, ferns and crops Earth. A clear winner makes the garden home ground for that element. A tie leaves it balanced. You can grow a place that suits your Cups without turning the rest of the world into an ocean.

The lantern shows the result in its flame's colour and movement. Its four sides take pottery sherds or suit fragments as clay designs. A Wands side is orange with a flame; a Cups side blue with a chalice. The lantern starts to look like yours, one side at a time.

## The Moon twice

At night, a ready garden's pond holds the actual Moon in its phase. Walk along the bank and the reflection follows your viewpoint; a low Moon draws a long path of glints towards you. Rain, a swimmer or a dropped item sends rings through it.

The pond also shows the current House's stars. At first only its brightest appear. Each lesson learned brings more into view, and a graduate's pond joins them into a constellation.

## Paper scholars

The **Star Guests** call themselves the **Quire**. A guest is folded paper about the size of a cat, cream outside and indigo within, with star eyes and leaves opening at its back. Its rank is visible in those leaves: **Folio**, **Quarto**, **Octavo**, **Duodecimo**. The last has twelve, one for each House.

<img src="../media/guest-ranks-day.webp" width="560" alt="The four ranks of Star Guest, from Folio to Duodecimo">

*Two leaves, four, eight, twelve.*

Their six lessons connect things already around you: **Pet, Tool, Table, Ground, Sky and Friend**. One reads your animal's chart with a paper Astrolabe. Another shows a card against your tool. A teacher beside three tables waits for you to complete an actual spread. Watching alone does not do the work for you.

You can discover these practices yourself; the Quire recognises learning by doing. For the Friend lesson, a villager you have traded with or the Astronomer can stand in when you play alone.

### The Troupe

On an Offering Plate set to **Call**, a card can fold into a new paper companion. Calling uses the card up; the ordinary **Show** setting doesn't. The guest plays one of twelve characters inspired by **commedia dell'arte** and its later cousins. Their masks and habits differ. The Commonplace introduces them without explaining their tricks:

You can carry, dress or offend a guest. When they're free, they may play with one another, borrow colour from a candle, pass it to a friend or wash it off in water. None of that makes them better at their jobs.

## Work at home

An **Offering Plate** is the garden's low wooden workstand. Set out the goods for an offering and a guest arrives to attend them; jacks can stand beside it holding supplies. The sky gives this craft its occasions, just as it does casting. The plate shows what it will do before anything is taken, or what it is still waiting for.

An animal can keep a card too. Preview a binding on your own tamed pet, then confirm to entrust it with that card. The Empress on a sitting cat tends nearby crops; the animal remains your cat, with an additional responsibility. Its temperament, read with the Astrolabe, helps explain which element keeps it good company.

Tuck the **Ace of Wands** into a pickaxe and its smelting follows the block you strike. The card's corner shows on the tool. Neither a familiar nor a tool consumes its card by casting, and both share its rest with you. The sky still has a say at the place where the work happens.

## Music disc

After all six lessons, the next Full Moon night brings graduation. Bring a blank card: the Quire prints your **soul card**, the Major of your Sun House, and gives you the music disc ***In Good Company***.

Put it in a jukebox. The Quire apparently expects a bit of dancing.

*"Tomorrow there is good work to do, but tonight there is good company."*
